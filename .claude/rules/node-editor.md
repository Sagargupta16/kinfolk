# Adding relatives on the canvas

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-10-31. Loads when a file matching `paths:` is read or edited.

## Key files

- [lib/tree/kin-plan.ts](lib/tree/kin-plan.ts) -- "add a father" as a PLAN: which union to find or create and what sex the role implies, one person per add. Also `splitName()`, the one name field split on its last word. Pure, because whether a father lands in the union that already holds the mother is a claim about a family.
- [lib/tree/node-add.ts](lib/tree/node-add.ts) -- the `+` handles as data: which ones the viewed person carries (`nodeAffordances`), where each sits on the laid-out card (`addSlots`), and its asymmetric 44px target (`hitBox`), plus where a strip opens from its control (`placeStrip`, `stripSide`). Pure, so a handle that covers a name or lands on the wrong family is asserted rather than eyeballed.
- [components/tree/NodeAdd.tsx](components/tree/NodeAdd.tsx) -- the handles, drawn through React Flow's viewport portal, and the add strip: a name, an optional birth year, and two gendered answers that are the submit buttons.
- [components/tree/NodeStrip.tsx](components/tree/NodeStrip.tsx) -- the one small strip both card actions open: no header and no sections, grown out of the control that opened it, moved by transform as the canvas pans, docked along the bottom of a narrow canvas.
- [components/tree/NodeEdit.tsx](components/tree/NodeEdit.tsx) -- the edit strip a second tap on the viewed card opens: name and year, Save, the full profile, and delete confirmed in place. Same write rules as PersonEdit.

## Gotchas

- **Adding a relative is relationship-first, and `planKin` in [lib/tree/kin-plan.ts](lib/tree/kin-plan.ts) is why it can be.** Since 0.6.0 the relationship is the POSITION of the `+` on the viewed card (see the node-editor gotchas below). Recording a father through the raw primitives took four steps and a schema lesson -- create person, create union, add partner, attach child -- and doing it twice for a father and a mother produced TWO single-parent unions, which draws two junctions and no couple. That is the worst class of bug here: it looks like a rendering fault, so nobody suspects the data. The planner finds an existing parent union with a free slot BEFORE creating one, which is the whole fix, and it is pure so the rule could be asserted rather than eyeballed (23 unit tests plus 10 live checks in `scripts/smoke-kin.mts`, all removed with the suite in the 0.2.0 rework).
- **The add strip has NO gender field: the two answers are gendered words, and the one pressed is the sex.** The canvas offers father/mother above a card, husband/wife beside it and son/daughter below it, and both answers are submit buttons, so recording a relationship records the sex in the same press. A posted `sex` still can never override a role that states one: `addRelative` honours it only when `ROLE_SEX[role] === "unknown"`, which now happens only for the neutral `partner`, `child` and `sibling` roles the API keeps for other callers. A value arriving alongside `role=father` is a forged post rather than a choice. Husband and wife are recorded as `married`, since the word says so. Deliberately NOT adopted from the research: FamilySearch's rule that sex cannot stay unspecified once somebody is linked as a spouse or parent, and Gramps' gender-filtered parent pickers -- both contradict a schema whose `sex` defaults to `unknown`.
- **The `+` handles are drawn for the VIEWED person only, through React Flow's `ViewportPortal`, and the portal paints UNDER the cards unless it is given a z-index.** Nodes carry `z-index: 2`, and the portal layer is a later sibling with no z-index of its own, so a handle drawn there sits under the card it belongs to and cannot be pressed. `.kf-node-add-layer` takes `z-index: 1002`, above the cards and the revealed relations (1001), with `pointer-events: none` on the layer and `all` on each button, `nodrag nopan` classes, and a stopped `pointerdown` so pressing a `+` does not pan. A couple's shared `+` sits in the gutter between the two cards, which no card owns -- that is why it is a portal and not a child of the card. One card wearing handles reads as "this is who you are editing"; handles on every card would be about three buttons a person (350 on the earlier 117-person sample), and a phone has no hover to reveal them. Hidden at the dot level and in orbit. A single-parent family keeps its own `+` under the card beside any couple's, because the couple's `+` would assert the spouse is the other parent. Which sides are free comes from the LAID-OUT row, not this tree's unions: in a combined view a spouse recorded only by the linked family sits in the partner gutter without being in them. The portal renders after every card, so Tab reaches the handles only after all 216 people in the sample; the `a` shortcut focuses the first one.
- **A `+` target is ASYMMETRIC, and centring it on the edge is what failed.** 44 screen pixels divided by the zoom is more than a third of a card at a legible zoom, so a centred target had to be capped to stop it covering the name -- and the cap won: 37x41 on cards and 42x23 on rows at fit zoom. `hitBox()` in [lib/tree/node-add.ts](lib/tree/node-add.ts) reaches only a fifth of the card inward and spends the rest of its height OUTWARD into the empty generation gap; its width stops short of the fold chevron, whose pill now sits at 16% across (`FOLD_AT`, matched in PersonNode); a side target reaches outward by less than the household gap so it cannot land on the next family's card. Every side is bounded by what it must not cover: OUTWARD by a little less than the gap between generations too, or a parent's bottom `+` covers the only child laid out directly beneath them and tapping that child opens "add a child" -- the first asymmetric version had exactly that overreach at low zoom, caught by review. A couple's target stops short of the RIGHT partner's fold pill, which sits 16% from the gutter side. Measured live: 44x44 at fit zoom on both card levels, no fold pill covered, every target the top element at its own centre, and zoomed out to 0.52, 0.3 and 0.17 no card centre under any target -- they shrink instead (25x27 at 0.3). A smaller target is a cost; a target that eclipses the next card is a defect.
- **Enter in the add strip walks the form; it never submits.** The two gendered answers are the form's submit buttons, and implicit submission presses the FIRST one -- so Enter after typing "Princi" would save her as a son. Enter moves name -> year -> the first answer, and saving is always a deliberate press. Verified live: two Enters left the person count unchanged.
- **A strip focuses its field when it is VISIBLE, not when it mounts.** `NodeStrip` renders its first frame with `visibility: hidden` until it is measured and placed, and `focus()` on a hidden element does nothing -- so the strips' own focus-on-mount left focus on the `+`, and the first letter typed went to the canvas shortcuts (a "d" changed the detail level and closed the strip). The field carries `data-autofocus` (`"select"` to select its text) and `NodeStrip` focuses it once placed. Docked on a phone, the strip also sits above `visualViewport`'s bottom, so the keyboard cannot cover it, and pans the canvas once if its card's `+` would be underneath.
- **A family choice is never pre-made, and its labels must tell the families apart.** When a parent slot is free in two families (birth and adoptive), or a person has two single-parent families, `planKin` refuses to guess -- so a radio checked by default would make the guess for it on every submit. The radios start empty and `required`, Enter on one walks on, and each label names the parents, the subject's parentage role and the children already there ("Pat (adoptive), with Casey"). Two different people can share a name -- two "Unknown" placeholders are ordinary -- so labels that still collide are numbered ("Unknown (1 of 2)").
- **Adding into a folded branch unfolds it, or the add looks like a failure.** A new parent of somebody whose ancestors are folded, or a child or partner of somebody whose descendants are, is written and then hidden by the fold -- the toast says added and the canvas shows nothing. Found by accident in the browser, where a mis-click folded a card. `revealAdded` opens the fold on success, and the PARTNER's descendants fold too for a couple's child, since folding descendants blocks the unions a person partners in.
- **After an add the viewed card holds still on screen, and framing must not undo that.** The handles belong to the viewed person and families are entered in runs ("Narendra's children are Paras and Princi"), so travelling to each new person would move the next `+` away; the new card flashes (`kf-new`) instead. But every write also ends in a relayout, and the framing effect frames every new `layoutEpoch` -- so a tree that fits ran `fitView` and slid the `+` from under the pointer, twice when the add opened a fold. `writeAnchor` in TreeCanvas records the viewed card's position before the write, and the layout that lands shifts the viewport by however far that card moved, in the SAME commit as the new positions (since 0.7.0; it used to wait for measurement, which left a frame of new positions under the old viewport -- invisible until cards started gliding). Measured: a card at screen (469, 486) was still at (469, 486) after a parent was added above it, and 0px of movement through a 0.7.0 birth. Changing the viewed person closes an open strip, so a strip for one person never sits beside another person's handles.
- **On an editable canvas a tap only PICKS; the profile waits to be asked for (0.7.0).** A tap used to open the profile too -- Family / About / Contact sections in a rail, or half a phone as a sheet -- so putting the `+` handles on somebody always brought a box of sections with it. `pick` in TreeCanvas now views and pins the person and opens nothing; a second tap on the viewed card opens the edit strip (or the profile, for a person whose rows belong to another family). The profile opens from the edit strip's Profile button, the People directory, the feed, and the `p` key; search only travels and selects (`goTo.profile` is false for it). A read-only canvas -- the demo, an invited viewer -- keeps tap-to-read, because there are no handles to reach and reading is what the visitor came for. `r` opens the edit strip from the keyboard.
- **A strip is anchored to its control, never centred, and has no header.** NodeStrip reads the live viewport transform, places itself beside the control on the side its relative goes (`placeStrip`, flipping when that side has no room, clamped with a 12px margin), and sets its transform-origin to the control's own point, so it grows out of the `+` and folds back into it. Its accessible name says whose relative or record it is; on screen the placeholder does ("Morgan and Alex's child" -- the longer "Name of ..." was cut off on both widths). Below 640px of canvas it docks along the bottom edge and still grows from the `+`. Positioned with Motion `x` / `y`, so following a pan is a transform. The open `+` turns into the close mark (a 45 degree turn) and a tap on the canvas closes it too, which is why the strip needs no close button.
- **A write's relayout is ONE storyboard, and every piece of it lives on the node's own element.** In the layout step, when a hold is active: every card that existed and changed place gets a FLIP (`kf-glide-0` / `kf-glide-1`, offsets in `--kf-glide-x/y`, measured on SCREEN, so the held card's offset is zero); the new relative gets `kf-born` with `--kf-from-x/y` pointing at the `+` it came from; the lines touching anything that changed get `kf-redraw-*` and draw back on as the cards come to rest. Three traps: React Flow owns the wrapper's transform, so every animation targets `.react-flow__node > *`; every combined animation list keeps `kf-enter` (and `kf-draw` on edges) FIRST with its exact original parameters, because React Flow reuses the DOM and a changed animation list would otherwise replay an entrance that finished long ago; and two keyframe names alternate, or a card moved by two writes in a row would glide only once. Clock: glide 440ms, lines from 260ms (`REDRAW_MS`), birth 120 to 680ms, its lines and any new junction at 560ms (`ARRIVE_MS`), ripple from 620ms -- under 1.5s, the svg-forge short preset. Measured live: the new card's first frame centred exactly on the `+` at 30% scale, landing through a 4% overshoot, resting transform `none`, 0 bad paths.
- **The handles' pop-in and pings belong to a PICK, not to a remount.** A write's relayout can remount the `+` buttons, and replaying the invitation then competed with the new relative's arrival. NodeAddControls opens a 5.4s intro window only when the subject changes (`kf-node-add-layer--intro`); outside it a remount simply appears. Verified: right after a pick the pills animate `kf-node-add-in`, and a write six seconds later leaves every pill at `animation-name: none`.
- **Deriving sex from a chosen ROLE is allowed; deriving it from a NAME is not.** The standing rule forbids reading "Maria" and storing `female`, because the user never said anything. Clicking "father" IS a statement about the person being added, so `ROLE_SEX` stores what they said -- and storing `unknown` instead would contradict them. `partner`, `child` and `sibling` stay neutral because those words genuinely imply nothing.
- **One named person per add, and the name is REQUIRED -- batches and numbered placeholders were removed in 0.6.0.** A batch existed because nobody knew the names yet, and it filled the canvas with "Son 1", "Son 2" cards that each needed renaming. `addRelative` now refuses a blank name before any write ("Type their name first."), like `addPerson`. The add strip's ONE name field is split by `splitName()` on its last word -- "Rakesh Kumar Gupta" is given name Rakesh Kumar, family name Gupta; a single word is a given name alone -- and the edit form posts `name` only when it was retyped, so a stored split the rule would not reproduce ("Jan" / "van der Berg") survives an untouched save. `buildPersonPatch` refuses a blank `name` rather than erasing it.
